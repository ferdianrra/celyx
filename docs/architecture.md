# Project structure

Features are grouped by the screen or workflow they own. There is no extra application-name folder under `Features`.

```text
Asterra/
  Features/
    Home/         Main screen, navigation, observation settings and astronomy state
    Garden/       Collected-object sky, collection lists, albums and story export
    Events/       Event data, reminder persistence, calendar and event details
    Sky/          Camera collection, detection, prediction overlays and photo processing
    Share/        Figma share cards, collection badges, PNG rendering and native share actions
  Shared/
    Style/        Colors, panels, reusable controls and hints
    Crosshair/    Crosshair view, animation model, feedback service and sounds
```

Each feature uses `Models`, `Views`, `ViewModels`, and `Services` as needed. Garden and Home also have `Rendering` for Metal implementations. Small presentation-only components do not need a separate view model.

- `HomeView` composes the main screen using the existing `HomeViewModel` for observation settings and collection data.
- `GardenViewModel` owns object placement, sensor lifecycle and camera gestures.
- The virtual garden places collected objects against the Milky Way environment. The capture handover uses that same backdrop and object placement.
- Garden constellation connections use the bundled figure edges and require both neighboring endpoints to be collected. No line is drawn to uncollected stars or arbitrary nearby objects. Existing connections remain on relaunch; new connections draw subtly toward the newly collected star over 0.9 seconds after its landing and focus finish, before the album opens. The virtual sky and live camera use their existing projection for each endpoint, and deletion removes the corresponding edges. Reduce Motion displays new connections immediately.
- Constellation threads have a soft blue-white halo and a fine luminous core. Every connected star has a staggered five-second timer and sends a gentle 2.4-second light sweep toward one collected neighbor, rotating among its connections. At most two sweeps run at once, and an edge never carries overlapping sweeps. Busy timers wait fairly for a free slot, so five seconds is the minimum start-to-start interval for a star rather than a strict global cadence. The animation timeline pauses between sweeps, and inactive gardens or Reduce Motion disable the ambient sweep entirely.
- Ambient sweeps activate only for connection lines that intersect the current frame, including partially visible segments. Offscreen constellations do not consume animation slots; looking away stops their sweeps. Looking back triggers the first sweep immediately, with the other stars staggered into their five-second cycles. This visibility rule follows the existing virtual or AR camera projection.
- Garden zodiac artwork is limited to Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpius, Sagittarius, Capricornus, Aquarius and Pisces. A figure unlocks only when every unique star ID in its bundled constellation line pattern is collected. Unlocks are derived from saved observations and disappear if a required star is deleted. The twelve prepared image sets in `Assets.xcassets/constellation-figures-rafi-aligned` follow the original three astronomical anchors in `Resources/sky/figures.json` through the garden/AR camera projection. A soft horizon mask (`GardenZodiacFigure.skyMask`, a small alpha image sampled from the true altitude) fades artwork in over about 2° above the horizon, following phone roll and the curved horizon of the stereographic dome. In both garden modes, artwork fades as the projected anchor triangle stretches unevenly compared with its centered view, as it nears the camera plane (a looser limit in the dome), as its centre sinks below the horizon, and as deep zoom makes it more than three to eight times the screen size; none of these limits makes it pop. This keeps the figure aligned to its anchors while it is visible. Camera roll and uniform zoom do not trigger the stretch fade. After the final star lands and the constellation is framed, the artwork gently fades in while settling from 104% to its size, with a brief glow, over 2.2 seconds; Reduce Motion shows it immediately. Previously completed figures do not replay on launch.
- Zodiac PNG decoding and glow preparation run once on a dedicated actor and are cached. Retained Core Animation layers reuse the decoded images; motion updates change transforms, opacity and the horizon mask. The reveal uses compositor opacity animations, with no figure animation timeline, image resolving or blur work in the gyro-driven draw path.
- Garden objects zoom like binoculars or a small telescope. Pinch reaches a 0.5° field of view in touch mode and 20× in gyro mode (`GardenZoomLimits`). The zoom eases toward the pinch and a released drag glides to a stop (`GardenViewModel.advanceMotion`); Reduce Motion applies both directly. Stars stay points of light when zoomed (at most 2.2× their size); the Sun, Moon and planets resolve into disks. `NightSeaRenderer` draws collected objects as instanced quads (`GardenBodyShader.txt`, appended to `NightSeaShader.txt` for the garden only; the wormhole keeps its point sprites). Stars use the original point-sprite appearance at every zoom, with a colored halo, four diffraction spikes and gentle pulsing. The Garden and wormhole share the same star shading function. Zoomed out, the Sun is an overexposed white disk with a warm glare. Planets keep the kit sprite. As the apparent disk grows, the Sun blends into the StarKit photosphere once zoomed in (like a solar filter), the Moon and planets into their garden textures on a lit, slowly rotating sphere, and Saturn into procedural bands and rings. Moon and planet lighting uses the real Sun direction and Astronomy Engine phase angle, so the Moon's phase and Venus's crescent are correct; night sides fade into the sky (the Moon keeps faint earthshine). The Moon and Sun never draw smaller than their true 0.27° radius, and every resolved disk is capped at 30% of the short screen side. `NightSeaObject.apparent` mirrors the shader sizes for label placement and tap targets.
- Navigation: a touch-mode pinch zooms toward the fingers (`GardenViewModel.zoomTouch`), pinch magnification is raised to the power 1.5, and dragging moves the sky 1:1 with the finger when zoomed in (1.15× at the wide default). In the virtual garden, object names fade in between garden zoom 2.0 and 2.6 and disappear when zoomed out; the live camera garden keeps showing them.
- Once the Sun is in the collection, the virtual garden's sky takes a thin wash of the real time of day from its live position: unchanged at night, a warm horizon on the Sun's side at dawn and dusk, and a faint blue sky with a glow around the Sun by day. The wash is capped at 35% (`NightSeaRenderer.daylightStrength`) so stars and the Milky Way stay visible, and it fades in over about two seconds (immediately with Reduce Motion). The wormhole sky is unchanged.
- The wide touch view uses a stereographic "dome" projection, like a planetarium: the horizon curves and more sky fits on screen without stretching the edges. `NightSeaCamera.stereographic` blends pinhole (0) and stereographic (1) and is mirrored in the sky and object shaders (`projectDir`/`rayDir`, passed as forward.w). The blend follows the touch field of view (full dome above 55°, pinhole below 15°, where both are nearly identical) and eases over a fraction of a second. Gyro mode, the live camera garden, the capture handover and the onboarding focus stay pinhole, so the real-sky window and the wormhole are unchanged.
- Hidden object names stay available to VoiceOver, and objects remain tappable. Both garden modes still respect the saved `gardenShowsStarNames` preference.
- Garden gyro tracking follows true north without touch offsets. A swipe preserves the current direction and zoom, then switches to touch; motion tracking continues quietly in the background. After a swipe, changing the phone’s aim by at least 5° while pointing near the horizon (−8°) or above resumes gyro after a brief 0.15-second debounce. Lowering the phone first is unnecessary. Small hand tremors and screen-axis roll do not trigger it; movement during a drag or pinch resets the reference so the gesture keeps control. The direction menu can also select either mode. GPS/compass readiness still gates real-world tracking, without displaying setup progress or calibration prompts in the garden.
- `StarCollectViewModel` owns target selection, capture verification, feedback and saving observations. `SkyFinderService` owns live camera analysis, matching and retained camera frames.
- `EventCollectionViewModel` owns the existing event-camera prototype state; it does not turn that prototype into real event detection.
- `SkyCalendarViewModel` owns date navigation and shared filters for the list, month grid and day chooser. All three hide events after their local viewing window ends. `SkyEventStore` owns reminder and collected-event persistence.
- `SkyCalendarView` binds the native segmented picker to the shared List/Calendar mode. The first-visit Calendar tip explains date browsing, event details and the reminder bell. The month header contains the month and navigation arrows.
- Sky event list/detail views share native reminder confirmation and result feedback. `SkyEventStore` changes bell state only after a successful system save/removal, persisting EventKit IDs alongside the existing reminder key. `SkyEventReminderService` owns Reminders access and EventKit operations. See [Sky event reminders](sky-event-reminders.md).

Views keep layout, gestures, sheets and navigation bindings. Models do not own screen layout. Services handle device APIs, image processing and persistence. Changes to folder structure do not change resource names, stored keys or catalog IDs.

The onboarding tour introduces the virtual collection space as Sky Garden. Back navigation resets pending validation and step progress before returning to the preceding interactive step. The camera-switch step replaces the main toolbar with Back and Skip Tour while keeping the highlighted bottom controls available. Start Collecting is dimmed and disabled while the tour points to AR, and becomes available when the camera is ready. The initial welcome, completion card and capture animation have no Back control. Note-sheet Back returns to the garden explanation without advancing to completion. Returning from the garden to the visibility choice releases the presentation without deleting saved observations. The welcome heading uses `asterra-app-logo`, a regular image set matching the current AppIcon PNG. The installed icon comes only from `Assets.xcassets/AppIcon.appiconset`; the legacy Icon Composer source is archived under `docs/design-assets/legacy-app-icon` outside the app target so it cannot override the catalog on newer iOS versions. When updating the icon, also refresh the onboarding image set from the default AppIcon PNG.

The first-run visibility choice is object-aware. During daytime it offers one guided-Sun action and places the Sun from the current time and location; it does not route through the ordinary optical camera choice. At night the real-camera action is the primary button above the virtual-star fallback. The guided target's filled direction arrow stays on a short orbit around the fixed capture reticle until the projected target approaches the lock area, including after it first enters the screen. The onboarding note editor relies on its focused field and descriptive, object-aware placeholder instead of adding a second instruction banner. Sun detail and share cards identify the roughly eight-minute light-travel time instead of using the generic solar-system subtitle.

Tapping a collected star opens `CollectedObjectSheet`; its Share button presents `StarShareView` full-screen. The same native detail sheet is used after a capture: once the focus sequence and return to the virtual garden orientation finish, the sheet starts with the native multiline SwiftUI TextField and keyboard focused. Done/Return saves the note, dismisses the keyboard and expands to the note/photo/metadata detail layout. Garden and collection-list taps open details directly. The pencil button enables inline note editing on the detail sheet. Close and sheet dismissal preserve draft notes, and deletion still requires native confirmation. Close returns to the same detail sheet. The page supports swipe selection between the Figma Polaroid and Full photo layouts. Cards use the saved photo, note, place and capture date; missing photos have an explicit unavailable state. STAR badges show the selected star’s chronological rank among the currently collected stars (including the Sun, excluding Moon/planets). Rank is derived from date with a stable ID tie-breaker, so deletion renumbers later stars without a storage migration. Badge styles are Silver for 0–49, Gold for 50–99 and Astungkara for 100+. Light pollution currently uses temporary `6.2/9` design data in `StarShareContent`, ready to receive a real rating. The same SwiftUI card renders a 1080×1920 image for Copy, Save to Photos and sharing. Instagram Stories and More use the handoffs described in [Social sharing](social-sharing.md), including transparent PNG export through More and access to WhatsApp via the iOS share sheet. The existing album/export code remains available for the capture-album flow.

On the demo branch, the collection camera starts in demo mode in Debug builds and TestFlight Release archives when opened through Start Collecting. The camera uses the standard guidance text and opens without a mode badge. Vega is available all day. The schedule uses the iPhone’s local time and includes the 25 Sagittarius line-pattern stars before noon and the 15 Aquarius line-pattern stars from noon until midnight. The set refreshes while the camera is open. Stars can be captured without optical detection, including below the horizon. Positions use the same HYG catalog, Astronomy Engine snapshot, refraction and camera alignment as the real sky. Aim downward if a star is below the horizon. The demo needs a location fix and camera tracking. Captures retain their observation date and location and identify the overlay as simulated. Repeat captures, shutter feedback, the Garden handover and Reduce Motion use the shared capture flow. Completed zodiac patterns unlock their artwork.

Capture handovers place the star, frame its collected neighbors or completed zodiac pattern (800 ms), reveal lines/artwork, hold for 2.7 seconds when a constellation line or figure is revealed or 700 ms for a star alone, and blend to the current virtual garden orientation over 1.1 seconds before opening the native note-entry sheet. The star alone is centered and focused when no pattern applies. Gyro updates continue during the presentation; touch gestures and home controls wait until the return completes. A successful capture switches to the virtual garden before the handover, including when collection started from the live camera garden. Closing the album no longer triggers a second star flight or reveal. Reduce Motion reveals in place and briefly pauses before the album, without a zoom/pan flight.

Sagittarius members are added to the collection when captured. Existing observations are preserved.

On this branch, both Garden views use the saved collection for constellation placement and artwork. The collection excludes photo-free Capricornus and nearby records marked as pre-collected by older demos. The records stay on disk for compatibility.

See `docs/zodiac-artwork.md` for editing artwork against the reference canvas. Garden artwork uses the asset catalog; other sky views retain their existing source figures.


## Repeat captures

The local `collectedStars` array stores one record per capture. Each record has a stable capture UUID and a separate catalog ID. Loading older records assigns and persists missing UUIDs. The latest capture for each catalog ID supplies the garden marker and collection entry. Constellation completion still uses the unique catalog IDs. A repeat capture keeps the existing lines and artwork visible during the handover.

Garden entries open a native object sheet with compact and large detents. Collection entries only use the large detent. The large detent shows all captures, newest first, and the lowest available Bortle class. Collection entries can also open camera guidance for the object. Guidance uses its current sky position, while saving still requires the camera's existing optical checks. Photo, note, place, capture conditions, and deletion are scoped to the capture UUID. Deleting the last capture removes the object from the garden.
